Every agent on this register has an ID, an owner who signed for it, and a record of what it has done. Paste an agent's ID, or type the name of an owner or an agent, to see who stands behind it and whether its record is clean.

An ID gives one of three answers: identified with a verified owner, identified with an owner on record, or unknown. A name finds owners who chose to show it. See every listed owner.

1. How the register works

  1. An owner signs for the agent. The owner is a company or a person. They hold a root key and sign each agent's ID with it. An ID with no owner's signature counts as unknown.
  2. Every action adds a receipt. Each time the agent acts through a verifier, the verifier adds a signed receipt to the agent's record: what kind of action, what the verdict was, and when. The agent's own software needs no change; the verifier it already acts through writes the receipt. The content of the action stays with the owner. The register holds only hashes.
  3. Anyone can check. Before your systems deal with an agent, look it up. The answer, the owner's signature and the whole receipt chain can be checked from the public keys alone. You do not have to trust this site.

2. What a determination means

Identified, owner verified
An owner signed for this agent and has proved who it is: it controls the domain it named (a token placed in the domain's DNS or on its website), or the register's keeper has reviewed it. A Companies House entry may be attached to a proved domain.
Identified, owner on record
An owner signed for this agent. It has not yet proved who it is.
Unknown
No agent with this ID is on the register. Treat it as anonymous.

What an agent may do with each answer is your decision. A bank might let an unknown agent read a price list and nothing more.

3. Entering your agents on the register

Anyone who owns an agent can enter it, in one command: npx @sheaf-labs/kya join <agent-name>. The tool makes the keys on your machine, you sign for the agent as its owner, and the register records it. Any system that runs or checks AI agents can also issue IDs and write receipts in the published format. The first one is Sheaf: every Sheaf account is on the register from the day it is made, each of its agents gets an ID under the owner's own key, and every checked action, past and future, is a receipt on the agent's record. An owner can hide its names or leave at any time. How to join.
